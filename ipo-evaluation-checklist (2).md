# IPO Evaluation Checklist — Features, Red Flags & What to Look For

A consolidated reference for assessing whether an IPO is likely to be a strong or weak investment. Most of this data is disclosed directly in the company's **RHP (Red Herring Prospectus)** — sections like Financial Statements, Risk Factors, Related Party Transactions, and Objects of the Issue.

---

## 1. Business & Financial Health

### Financial track record (revenue, profit, margins over 3–5 yrs)
- **Why it matters:** Shows if the business actually makes money sustainably, not just growing on paper.
- **Green flag:** Consistent revenue growth, stable or improving margins.
- **Red flag:** Revenue growth but widening losses with no clear path to profitability.
- **What to look for:** 5-year restated financial statements in the RHP. Check margin trend (gross, EBITDA, net) — improving or deteriorating? Compare growth rate to sector average.

### Cash flow quality vs. reported profit
- **Why it matters:** Profit on paper (P&L) isn't the same as cash actually collected.
- **Green flag:** Operating cash flow tracks closely with, or exceeds, net profit.
- **Red flag:** Profits rising steadily while operating cash flow is flat, negative, or lagging — often means revenue is stuck in receivables.
- **What to look for:** Compare "cash flow from operations" to "net profit" line by line across years in the cash flow statement.

### Working capital cycle
- **Why it matters:** Shows how efficiently the company converts operations into cash.
- **Green flag:** Stable or shortening cycle (faster collections, controlled inventory).
- **Red flag:** Lengthening cycle — slower collections or bloated inventory, even if revenue still looks fine.
- **What to look for:** Days sales outstanding, days inventory outstanding, days payable — trend over 3 years, in the MD&A section.

### Debt levels / use of proceeds for debt repayment
- **Why it matters:** High debt funded by IPO money can mean the business was financially stretched pre-IPO.
- **Green flag:** Low leverage, or a meaningful, clear reduction in debt from IPO proceeds.
- **Red flag:** IPO used mainly to repay debt that keeps recurring (repeated refinancing pattern).
- **What to look for:** Debt-to-equity ratio trend, interest coverage ratio, and the "Objects of the Issue" breakdown.

### Contingent liabilities
- **Why it matters:** Hidden risk not reflected in the headline balance sheet.
- **Green flag:** Contingent liabilities are small relative to net worth.
- **Red flag:** Large disputed tax demands, guarantees, or pending claims relative to net worth.
- **What to look for:** Notes to financial statements — usually a dedicated "Contingent Liabilities" table.

---

## 2. Deal Structure

### Purpose of the issue (fresh issue vs. OFS)
- **Why it matters:** Fresh issue money builds the business; a pure Offer for Sale (OFS) just lets existing insiders cash out.
- **Green flag:** Meaningful fresh issue component for expansion, capex, or debt reduction.
- **Red flag:** Large OFS with promoters/PE investors exiting heavily, little to no fresh capital for the company.
- **What to look for:** Issue structure table on the cover of the RHP — split between fresh issue and OFS.

### Objects of the issue — how specific are they?
- **Why it matters:** Specificity signals accountability for how your money will be used.
- **Green flag:** Itemized, specific plans (e.g., "₹200 cr for new manufacturing capacity").
- **Red flag:** A large percentage earmarked for vague "general corporate purposes."
- **What to look for:** "Objects of the Issue" section — check the % allocated to unspecified use.

### Valuation vs. peers (P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B)
- **Why it matters:** Tells you if you're overpaying relative to comparable listed companies.
- **Green flag:** Priced in line with or below listed peers, with a reasonable growth premium if any.
- **Red flag:** Priced well above listed peers with no clear justification (moat, growth rate, margins).
- **What to look for:** "Basis for Issue Price" section in the RHP — compares the company's ratios to listed peers directly.

### Issue size relative to market cap (dilution %)
- **Why it matters:** A large issue relative to implied market cap means heavy dilution and more supply for the market to absorb.
- **Green flag:** Moderate dilution, issue size proportionate to company scale.
- **Red flag:** Very large issue size relative to post-listing market cap — can pressure the stock post-listing.
- **What to look for:** Calculate (issue size ÷ post-issue market cap). Compare to typical dilution levels (usually 10–25%) for similar-sized IPOs.

### Lock-in periods for pre-IPO investors
- **Why it matters:** When lock-ins expire, large blocks of shares can hit the market and create sell pressure.
- **Green flag:** Staggered, longer lock-ins across promoter and pre-IPO investor categories.
- **Red flag:** Short lock-ins on large pre-IPO stakes — a looming supply overhang.
- **What to look for:** "Capital Structure" section — lock-in schedule by shareholder category.

### Employee Stock Option (ESOP) overhang
- **Why it matters:** Large unvested/unexercised pools mean a steady stream of new shares hitting the market over time.
- **Green flag:** Modest, well-vested ESOP pool.
- **Red flag:** Large unvested pool concentrated to vest soon after listing.
- **What to look for:** ESOP scheme disclosures — total pool size, vesting schedule, % already exercised.

## 3. Ownership & Governance

### Promoter holding post-IPO
- **Why it matters:** Skin in the game signals confidence in the business.
- **Green flag:** Promoters retain a large stake after listing.
- **Red flag:** Promoters dilute down to a small minority stake.
- **What to look for:** Pre- and post-issue shareholding pattern table in the RHP.

### Promoter share pledging
- **Why it matters:** Pledged shares mean promoters are financially stretched and forced selling can worsen a price decline.
- **Green flag:** No or minimal promoter share pledging.
- **Red flag:** A large % of promoter holding pledged as loan collateral.
- **What to look for:** Shareholding pattern disclosures, and later, quarterly shareholding filings on NSE/BSE post-listing.

### Related-party transactions
- **Why it matters:** Can indicate value being siphoned to promoter-linked entities.
- **Green flag:** Minimal, transparent, arm's-length related-party dealings.
- **Red flag:** Frequent, large, or opaque transactions with promoter-owned entities.
- **What to look for:** "Related Party Transactions" note in the financial statements — value as a % of revenue/expenses.

### Corporate governance history
- **Why it matters:** Past behavior is a strong predictor of future behavior.
- **Green flag:** Clean audit history, independent board representation, stable leadership.
- **Red flag:** Auditor resignations, past financial restatements, regulatory action against promoters/directors.
- **What to look for:** "Risk Factors" section (companies must disclose past regulatory actions) and auditor's report history.

### Historical restatement of financials
- **Why it matters:** Repeated restatements can indicate weak internal controls or prior aggressive accounting.
- **Green flag:** No restatements, or only minor technical ones.
- **Red flag:** Multiple restatements in the years leading up to the IPO.
- **What to look for:** Auditor's notes and restated financial statements section — compare against previously filed financials if available.

### Management / key managerial personnel background
- **Why it matters:** Leadership stability and track record affect execution risk.
- **Green flag:** Experienced team, low churn, no history of regulatory issues.
- **Red flag:** Frequent CFO/CEO changes shortly before the IPO, or past regulatory action against management.
- **What to look for:** "Management" section — tenure of key executives, past directorships, disciplinary history.

## 4. Business Quality & Risk

### Revenue/customer concentration risk
- **Why it matters:** Heavy reliance on one client, product, or geography is fragile.
- **Green flag:** Diversified customer base and revenue streams.
- **Red flag:** Top 1–10 customers make up a very large % of revenue.
- **What to look for:** "Top customers as % of revenue" disclosure, usually in the business overview or risk factors section.

### Litigation / regulatory risk factors
- **Why it matters:** Legal issues can materially hit future earnings or operations.
- **Green flag:** Minimal pending litigation relative to company size.
- **Red flag:** Multiple or large pending cases against the company or its promoters.
- **What to look for:** "Outstanding Litigation" section — count and aggregate value of cases by category (criminal, tax, civil, regulatory).

### Market share and competitive moat
- **Why it matters:** A good company in a shrinking or commoditized sector still struggles long-term.
- **Green flag:** Category leadership, pricing power, defensible moat (brand, licenses, scale, tech).
- **Red flag:** Small player in a crowded, price-competitive space with no differentiation.
- **What to look for:** Industry section of the RHP (often has an independent market research report attached), competitor comparison.

### Industry/sector tailwinds
- **Why it matters:** Even a well-run company needs a growing addressable market.
- **Green flag:** Growing sector, structural demand tailwinds, low disruption risk.
- **Red flag:** Declining or highly cyclical sector, easily disrupted by new technology/entrants.
- **What to look for:** Industry overview section, and independent third-party industry reports cited in the RHP.

### Regulatory/sector-specific risk
- **Why it matters:** Heavily regulated sectors (NBFCs, insurance, telecom, pharma) are exposed to sudden policy shifts.
- **Green flag:** Stable regulatory environment, no major pending policy changes flagged.
- **Red flag:** Pending regulatory changes disclosed as a risk that could materially affect the business.
- **What to look for:** "Risk Factors" section, specifically regulatory/government policy risk disclosures.

### Dividend history / capital allocation discipline
- **Why it matters:** Indicates financial discipline and confidence in sustained cash generation (less critical for high-growth companies).
- **Green flag:** History of sustainable dividends, or a clear, credible reinvestment story if dividends are absent.
- **Red flag:** No dividends and no credible explanation of where the cash is going.
- **What to look for:** Dividend history table in the RHP, and cash flow statement to see how retained earnings are deployed.

## 5. Market Sentiment & Demand Signals

### Anchor investor quality
- **Why it matters:** Reputed institutional anchors have already done due diligence before you invest.
- **Green flag:** Strong, diverse anchor book (large mutual funds, sovereign funds, well-known institutions).
- **Red flag:** Anchor book missing entirely, or filled with unknown/small players.
- **What to look for:** Anchor investor allotment list, published a day before the issue opens (on exchange websites).

### Subscription levels (QIB / HNI / Retail)
- **Why it matters:** Reflects real institutional and market confidence beyond marketing hype.
- **Green flag:** Heavy QIB oversubscription (institutions doing deep diligence are buying in).
- **Red flag:** Retail-only interest with QIBs lukewarm or under-subscribed.
- **What to look for:** Live subscription data on NSE/BSE during the bidding window, broken down by investor category.

### Grey Market Premium (GMP)
- **Why it matters:** An informal, unregulated indicator of expected listing-day sentiment — useful as a rough gauge, not a fundamental signal.
- **Green flag:** Positive and relatively stable GMP in the days leading to listing.
- **Red flag:** Volatile, sharply falling, or negative GMP close to listing.
- **What to look for:** Treat as directional only — cross-check with subscription data and peer valuation, since GMP is unofficial and can be manipulated.

### Broader market conditions at time of listing
- **Why it matters:** Even a fundamentally sound company can list poorly in a weak market, and vice versa — this is timing risk, separate from company quality.
- **Green flag:** Listing during a period of healthy risk appetite and stable broader markets.
- **Red flag:** Listing during a market downturn, high volatility, or negative macro news flow.
- **What to look for:** Broader index trend (Nifty/Sensex) and recent IPO listing performance in the weeks before the issue.

## 6. Independent Financial Analysis (beyond the RHP)

### Independent ratio benchmarking
- **Why it matters:** RHP peer comparisons are chosen by the company/bankers — worth picking your own peer set.
- **What to look for:** Calculate ROE, ROCE, ROIC, asset turnover yourself using tools like Screener.in, Tijori, or Moneycontrol, and compare to the best-in-class peers, not just the ones the RHP lists.

### Independent DCF / intrinsic valuation
- **Why it matters:** Cross-checks the RHP's implied "fair value" against your own assumptions.
- **What to look for:** Build a basic DCF with conservative growth/margin assumptions and see how far the IPO price is from your intrinsic value estimate.

### MCA/RoC filings cross-check
- **Why it matters:** RHP numbers should match what's filed with the Registrar of Companies — mismatches are serious.
- **What to look for:** Pull financials from mca.gov.in and compare against RHP disclosures.

### Credit rating agency reports (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE)
- **Why it matters:** An independent third-party view of financial risk, not company-authored.
- **What to look for:** Read the full rating rationale report, not just the letter grade — it's often more candid about risks than the RHP.

### Broker/analyst independent IPO notes
- **Why it matters:** Sell-side and independent research desks do their own diligence and sometimes catch things the RHP glosses over.
- **What to look for:** Read notes from 3–4 different brokerages and pay attention to where they disagree, not just where they agree.

### Promoter group's other companies
- **Why it matters:** Financial stress in other group companies can bleed into this one via loans, guarantees, or related-party flows.
- **What to look for:** Search for other companies under the same promoter/group name and check their financial health and any default history.

### Sector-specific external data
- **Why it matters:** Company-reported industry numbers can be optimistic.
- **What to look for:** Cross-check industry size/growth claims against independent sources — RBI reports, CMIE, government ministry data, global research firms.

### Peer IPO post-listing performance
- **Why it matters:** How similarly-profiled companies from the same sector performed after listing is a useful base rate.
- **What to look for:** Look at the last 3–5 IPOs in the same sector and whether they sustained their price or faded after listing.

## 7. Technical / Market-Based Signals

### Broader market trend (Nifty/Sensex)
- **Why it matters:** IPOs launched during strong bull runs get inflated demand regardless of fundamentals, and vice versa.
- **What to look for:** Index trend over the preceding 1–3 months — rising, falling, or choppy.

### India VIX (volatility index)
- **Why it matters:** High volatility means a more uncertain, riskier listing environment.
- **What to look for:** VIX above ~18–20 generally signals nervous markets; lower VIX means calmer listing conditions.

### FII/DII flow trends
- **Why it matters:** Sustained institutional buying/selling shapes overall market liquidity for new issues.
- **What to look for:** Recent FII/DII net flow data (NSE, Moneycontrol) — persistent FII selling is a headwind for IPO demand.

### Sector index momentum
- **Why it matters:** A company's own sector index trend often matters more than the broad market.
- **What to look for:** E.g., for a bank/NBFC IPO, check Nifty Bank/Nifty Financial Services trend, not just Nifty 50.

### Recent IPO market cycle/sentiment
- **Why it matters:** IPO markets run in cycles — sometimes almost everything lists at a premium, sometimes almost everything lists flat or negative.
- **What to look for:** Track the last 10–15 IPOs' listing-day performance to gauge the prevailing base rate of the current cycle.

### Global cues
- **Why it matters:** Relevant for sectors linked to global markets — IT, pharma exports, commodities.
- **What to look for:** Dollar index, crude oil prices, US Fed rate decisions, global sector peer performance.

### IPO grading (if available)
- **Why it matters:** An independent grading from a rating agency, separate from the credit rating, though optional since 2013 and rarely done now.
- **What to look for:** Check if any rating agency has published a grade for the issue.

### Listing-day volume and price action
- **Why it matters:** Early trading behavior reveals real demand vs. speculative flipping.
- **What to look for:** First-day volume relative to issue size, and whether price holds up in the 1–2 weeks after listing-day volatility settles.

## 8. Deal Mechanics & Intermediaries

### Lead manager / Book Running Lead Manager (BRLM) track record
- **Why it matters:** A top-tier BRLM does deeper due diligence, prices issues more realistically, and has more credibility with institutional investors — a weak or inexperienced BRLM is a proxy for weaker overall deal quality.
- **Green flag:** Reputed BRLM(s) with a strong history of issues that priced fairly and held up post-listing.
- **Red flag:** Little-known merchant banker, or a BRLM with a track record of past IPOs that listed poorly or attracted SEBI scrutiny.
- **What to look for:** BRLM names on the RHP cover page; search each BRLM's past mandates and how those IPOs performed 3–6 months after listing, not just on day one.

### Book building vs. fixed-price issue
- **Why it matters:** Book-built issues let the market discover price within a band via real demand; fixed-price issues rely entirely on the company/banker's own valuation with no live price discovery.
- **Green flag:** Book-built issue with a reasonably tight price band and broad-based bidding across categories.
- **Red flag:** Fixed-price issue (more common in small SME IPOs) with no independent price discovery mechanism.
- **What to look for:** Issue structure section of the RHP; for book-built issues, how much the final cut-off price moved within the band.

### Price band width
- **Why it matters:** A very wide band signals the company/banker aren't confident about fair value; a tight band suggests more conviction in the pricing.
- **Green flag:** Narrow price band (small gap between floor and cap price).
- **Red flag:** Unusually wide band, leaving a lot of ambiguity about what investors are actually paying for.
- **What to look for:** Price band disclosure in the RHP/final prospectus.

### Green shoe option (price stabilisation mechanism)
- **Why it matters:** A green shoe option gives the lead manager a SEBI-permitted tool to buy back shares in the market for up to 30 days if the stock falls below issue price, cushioning early volatility.
- **Green flag:** Green shoe option included, with a credible stabilising agent.
- **Red flag:** No stabilisation mechanism in a highly-priced or richly-valued issue — more downside risk is left completely unmanaged in the first month.
- **What to look for:** "Green Shoe Option" / stabilisation clause in the RHP; post-listing stabilisation fund disclosures on exchange filings if the option is exercised.

### Issue expenses / cost of raising capital
- **Why it matters:** High issue expenses (fees, commissions, marketing) eat into the actual proceeds that reach the business, especially relevant for smaller issues.
- **Green flag:** Issue expenses at a reasonable % of issue size (typically low single digits for large issues).
- **Red flag:** Disproportionately high issue expenses relative to issue size, common in some small/SME IPOs.
- **What to look for:** "Issue Expenses" breakdown, usually near the "Objects of the Issue" section.

### Employee reservation / shareholder quota
- **Why it matters:** A meaningful employee quota with take-up suggests internal confidence in the business.
- **Green flag:** Employees participate meaningfully at the discounted employee price.
- **Red flag:** Employee portion under-subscribed or employees visibly not participating despite a discount.
- **What to look for:** Category-wise subscription data (employee reservation category) during the bidding window.

## 9. Macro & Regulatory Environment

### Interest rate cycle
- **Why it matters:** Rising rates increase the discount rate used in valuations and make debt costlier, pressuring both IPO pricing and post-listing performance, especially for capital-intensive or high-growth/high-multiple businesses.
- **Green flag:** Stable or falling rate environment, supportive of risk-taking and higher multiples.
- **Red flag:** Rising-rate cycle, especially for richly-valued or debt-heavy issuers.
- **What to look for:** RBI policy rate trajectory and central bank commentary around the listing window.

### Inflation trends
- **Why it matters:** High inflation compresses margins for input-cost-sensitive businesses and can dampen consumer demand, hurting consumption-facing IPOs.
- **Green flag:** Benign, stable inflation supportive of margins and demand.
- **Red flag:** High or rising inflation, particularly relevant for manufacturing, FMCG, and consumer discretionary issuers.
- **What to look for:** CPI/WPI trends and company-specific commentary on input cost pass-through in the RHP's risk factors and MD&A.

### Currency / forex exposure
- **Why it matters:** Companies with import-heavy costs or export-heavy revenue are directly exposed to currency swings, which can swing reported profitability independent of core operations.
- **Green flag:** Natural hedge (revenue and costs in the same currency) or disclosed active hedging policy.
- **Red flag:** Large unhedged forex exposure — e.g., rupee-denominated debt with dollar revenue mismatch, or vice versa.
- **What to look for:** "Risk Factors" section on forex, and forex derivative/hedging disclosures in the financial notes.

### Sector-specific regulatory or policy risk
- **Why it matters:** A sudden policy change (tariffs, licensing rules, price caps, environmental norms) can materially alter a business's economics overnight, especially in regulated sectors.
- **Green flag:** Stable, predictable regulatory regime with no major pending changes flagged.
- **Red flag:** Sector facing an active regulatory overhaul, new compliance cost, or government policy uncertainty (e.g., telecom spectrum rules, NBFC lending norms, pharma pricing caps).
- **What to look for:** Government ministry/regulator announcements for the sector, and the "Government and Regulatory Approvals" and risk sections of the RHP.

### IPO pipeline / market supply glut
- **Why it matters:** When too many IPOs hit the market in a short window, investor liquidity gets spread thin, which can suppress subscription levels and listing performance across the board, independent of any single company's quality.
- **Green flag:** Issue launching in a relatively uncrowded IPO calendar window.
- **Red flag:** Issue launching alongside several large, competing IPOs raising capital in the same weeks.
- **What to look for:** Exchange IPO calendar for overlapping issue/bidding windows around the same dates.

### Global macro cues (for globally-linked sectors)
- **Why it matters:** IT, pharma exports, commodities, and auto-ancillary businesses are directly affected by global demand, currency, and trade conditions.
- **Green flag:** Supportive global demand backdrop and stable trade environment for the sector.
- **Red flag:** Global slowdown, trade restrictions, tariff escalation, or a strengthening dollar hurting the sector's economics.
- **What to look for:** Global sector peer commentary, US Fed policy, and trade/tariff news relevant to the company's key export or import markets.

## 10. Structural, Behavioral & Post-Listing Risk Factors

### Allotment probability / oversubscription mechanics
- **Why it matters:** Extremely high oversubscription in the retail category means a very low chance of actually getting allotted shares, which changes the practical risk-reward of applying versus buying post-listing.
- **Green flag:** Moderate oversubscription with a reasonable, calculable allotment probability.
- **Red flag:** Retail oversubscription so extreme that allotment is effectively a lottery, encouraging pure speculative "apply and flip" behavior rather than genuine investment demand.
- **What to look for:** Category-wise subscription multiples and the lot-size/allotment ratio disclosed by the registrar (RTA) after bidding closes.

### Flipping behavior / listing-day supply overhang
- **Why it matters:** A large share of allottees who intend to sell immediately on listing (rather than hold) creates artificial early selling pressure regardless of fundamentals.
- **Green flag:** Broad-based, genuine long-term demand signaled by steady post-listing volumes rather than a single-day dump.
- **Red flag:** GMP-driven retail frenzy with little institutional anchoring — a setup for a "sell on listing" stampede.
- **What to look for:** Compare listing-day volume to total float, and watch price action over the following 1–2 weeks, not just the opening print.

### Key-person / founder dependency risk
- **Why it matters:** Businesses heavily dependent on a single founder, promoter, or a handful of key executives for relationships, technical know-how, or vision carry higher execution risk if that person exits or is incapacitated.
- **Green flag:** Institutionalised processes, a deep management bench, low dependency on any one individual.
- **Red flag:** Business built almost entirely around one founder's personal relationships, brand, or expertise, with no clear succession plan.
- **What to look for:** "Management" and "Risk Factors" sections — explicit disclosure of key-man dependency, succession planning, and any non-compete/key-man insurance arrangements.

### Auditor changes / auditor quality
- **Why it matters:** A recent change in auditor, especially shortly before the IPO, can be an innocuous rotation or a red flag for disagreements over accounting treatment.
- **Green flag:** Long-tenured, reputed audit firm with no recent unexplained changes.
- **Red flag:** Auditor resigned or was replaced shortly before the IPO, especially without a clear, disclosed reason.
- **What to look for:** Auditor history and any resignation letters/qualifications disclosed in the financial statements section.

### Customer/supplier contract concentration and renewal risk
- **Why it matters:** Even a diversified-looking revenue base can be fragile if key contracts are up for renewal soon or lack long-term commitments.
- **Green flag:** Long-duration contracts, high renewal rates, sticky customer relationships.
- **Red flag:** Short-tenure contracts with major customers/suppliers coming up for renewal shortly after listing, with no guaranteed continuation.
- **What to look for:** Contract duration disclosures in the business overview section, and any specific renewal-risk callouts in "Risk Factors."

### Technology / disruption risk
- **Why it matters:** Businesses exposed to fast-moving technology shifts (AI, automation, fintech disintermediation, etc.) can see their competitive position erode quickly even with a currently strong balance sheet.
- **Green flag:** Demonstrated ability to adapt to technology shifts, ongoing R&D/tech investment, or a business model that's structurally hard to disrupt.
- **Red flag:** Business model vulnerable to an already-visible technology or platform shift, with little evidence of adaptation.
- **What to look for:** Industry overview section for disruption commentary, and R&D/technology spend trend in the financials.

### ESG and sustainability disclosures
- **Why it matters:** Weak environmental, social, or governance practices can create future compliance costs, reputational risk, or exclusion from ESG-focused institutional portfolios.
- **Green flag:** Credible ESG disclosures, reasonable compliance track record on labor/environmental norms.
- **Red flag:** History of environmental violations, labor disputes, or governance lapses flagged in risk factors.
- **What to look for:** "Risk Factors" and any BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) disclosures included with the RHP.

### Promoter/group company financial stress and cross-guarantees
- **Why it matters:** Financial trouble in a promoter's other (unlisted) group companies can bleed into the IPO company via inter-company loans, corporate guarantees, or diverted management attention.
- **Green flag:** Clean, ring-fenced structure with no material guarantees or loans to/from group entities.
- **Red flag:** Sizeable loans, guarantees, or cash flows between the IPO company and financially stressed group entities.
- **What to look for:** Related-party transaction notes and "Risk Factors" disclosures on group company exposure.

### SEBI observations / DRHP-to-RHP changes and delays
- **Why it matters:** A long gap or multiple rounds of queries between the draft (DRHP) filing and final RHP can indicate SEBI raised material concerns that had to be addressed.
- **Green flag:** Relatively smooth, timely SEBI clearance with no major disclosure changes between DRHP and RHP.
- **Red flag:** Repeated SEBI observation letters, long delays, or significant last-minute changes to disclosures between DRHP and RHP.
- **What to look for:** SEBI's public "Observations" letters (available on sebi.gov.in against the company's DRHP filing) and a side-by-side comparison of DRHP vs. RHP disclosures if both are available.

### Post-listing lock-in and share-pledge monitoring
- **Why it matters:** Risk doesn't end at listing — promoter pledging, further stake sales, or lock-in expiries months later can still move the stock materially.
- **Green flag:** Promoter commits to (and follows through on) not pledging shares; no signs of stake reduction beyond disclosed lock-in schedules.
- **Red flag:** Promoter pledges shares soon after listing, or sells beyond what was disclosed pre-IPO.
- **What to look for:** Quarterly shareholding pattern filings on NSE/BSE for at least 2–4 quarters after listing.

---

**Mental model:** the RHP tells you what the company *is*; independent financial analysis tells you if that's actually a fair story; market/technical signals tell you if the *timing* is right; and deal mechanics, macro conditions, and post-listing behavior tell you how the *process itself* can distort outcomes even when the business and timing are both sound. All these can point in different directions — a great company can have bad IPO timing or a distorted process, and a mediocre company can list well simply because the market cycle is hot.

---

## Quick reference: where in the RHP to look

| Section of RHP | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| Risk Factors | Litigation, regulatory risk, concentration risk, governance issues |
| Financial Statements & Notes | Financial track record, cash flow quality, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions |
| Objects of the Issue | Use of proceeds, fresh issue vs. OFS split |
| Basis for Issue Price | Valuation vs. peers |
| Capital Structure | Shareholding pattern, promoter holding, lock-in schedule |
| Management | Key personnel background, board composition |
| Industry Overview | Sector tailwinds, market size, competitive landscape |
| Outstanding Litigation | Pending legal/regulatory cases |
| Cover page (BRLM names) | Lead manager/BRLM identity for track-record research |
| Issue Expenses | Cost of raising capital as % of issue size |
| Related Party Transactions (notes) | Promoter group cross-guarantees/loans, group company stress |
| Government & Regulatory Approvals | Sector-specific regulatory/policy risk |
| SEBI Observations (sebi.gov.in) | DRHP-to-RHP changes, delays, disclosure concerns |

*Not investment advice — this is a framework for structuring your own research, not a recommendation on any specific IPO.*
